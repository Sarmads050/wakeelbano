Injunction and Stay Orders (Order XXXIX CPC)

How injunctions work in Pakistan: the three tests under Order XXXIX CPC, temporary and permanent injunction, status quo orders, and the drafting that gets a stay granted.

A court grants a temporary injunction under Order XXXIX CPC when the plaintiff shows a prima facie case, irreparable loss that cannot be compensated in money, and that the balance of convenience favours protecting the property or the position until the suit is decided.

Key facts

Statute
Code of Civil Procedure 1908, Order XXXIX rules 1 and 2
Three tests
Prima facie case, irreparable loss, balance of convenience
Types
Temporary (during the suit) and permanent (in the decree)
Speed matters
Delay in applying is itself an argument against the relief
Practical form
Status quo orders are the most common relief granted
Cost
Court fee on the application, plus an ad interim order that can be appealed

What are the three tests a court applies?

Prima facie case, irreparable injury, and the balance of convenience — all three, not just one.

TestWhat it means in practice
Prima facie caseThe documents show a real question to try — not a certain win, but more than a suspicion
Irreparable injuryHarm that money cannot fix, such as construction, demolition, or transfer to a third party
Balance of convenienceWho suffers more if the court gets it wrong — usually settled by maintaining the existing position
The most common outcome in Pakistan is a status quo order: nobody changes the position on the ground until the suit is decided. Ask for it in clear words in the application.

How is the application drafted?

Facts, the right relied on, the three tests with documents, the urgency, and a prayer that says exactly what must not happen.

  1. Title and application number — in the pending suit, under Order XXXIX rules 1 and 2.
  2. Facts — dated and short, with the documents that prove the right.
  3. Prima facie case — a separate paragraph, referring to specific annexures.
  4. Irreparable loss — say what will be lost and why money is not enough.
  5. Balance of convenience — show that the other side loses nothing by waiting, and you lose everything.
  6. Conduct and delay — explain any gap between the cause of action and the application.
  7. Prayer — "restrain the defendant from … selling / constructing / alienating / dispossessing" and status quo.
  8. Affidavit and annexures — a supporting affidavit, properly attested.

Why are injunction applications refused?

Delay, no clear right, disputed questions of fact that need trial, and a prayer that is too vague to enforce.

  • Delay — a plaintiff who waits six months has answered the urgency question himself.
  • No demonstrable right — the documents do not show title, possession or a concluded agreement.
  • Money is enough — where compensation can fix the loss, an injunction usually will not.
  • Vague prayer — an order the defendant cannot understand is an order the court will not pass.
  • Concealment — an application that hides an earlier suit or an earlier transfer is dismissed with costs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a temporary and a permanent injunction?

A temporary injunction under Order XXXIX protects the position while the suit is pending. A permanent injunction is granted in the decree itself after trial, and is the final relief.

Can an injunction order be appealed?

An order granting or refusing a temporary injunction is appealable under Order XLIII CPC. Check the appeal route and limitation immediately if the order goes against your client.

What if the other side disobeys the order?

Apply for contempt and for attachment under Order XXXIX rule 2-A. Keep a dated record of the breach, with photographs and witnesses.
