Civil and Property Litigation in Pakistan

The four suits that carry most civil practice in Pakistan: specific performance of an agreement to sell, injunction and stay orders, execution of a decree, and partition of joint property. Procedure, documents and drafting.

Most civil litigation in Pakistan turns on four suits: specific performance to force a sale that was agreed but not completed, an injunction to stop the other side acting while the suit runs, execution to actually recover what a decree gave you, and partition to divide jointly owned property. Each has its own limitation, court fee and document bundle.

What decides which of these suits you file?

What the client actually wants: the property, the money, protection while the case runs, or a clean division.

What the client wantsThe suitLimitation to check first
The property they agreed to buySpecific performanceRuns from the date fixed for performance
The other side stopped from actingInjunction / stay orderWith the main suit; delay is fatal to the relief
To actually get the money or possession a decree gaveExecutionRuns from the date of the decree
A defined share instead of joint ownershipPartitionWhen the plaintiff is denied his share
Limitation is where these cases are lost. Before drafting anything, open the Limitation Act 1908 and fix the date the clock started. Then decide the court fee, because a suit undervalued will be returned at scrutiny.

What documents should the file contain before you draft?

The document that proves the right, proof of payment, the notice, and a current certificate of title or revenue record.

  • The agreement or title document — sale agreement, sale deed, allotment letter, lease, or the revenue record.
  • Proof of payment — receipts, bank transfers, cheques, and the seller's written acknowledgement.
  • The notice — the demand made, and proof it was delivered.
  • Title and encumbrance — current fard, registry extract, and any prior litigation on the property.
  • Correspondence — messages and letters showing what each side agreed and refused.

Frequently asked questions

Do property suits always go to the civil court?

Usually the civil court of the district where the property is situated, but revenue matters, rent matters and family property disputes can go elsewhere. Check jurisdiction and forum first.

How long does a civil suit take in Karachi?

A contested property suit commonly runs several years through the trial court and the first appeal. Complete documents and a clean valuation shorten it more than anything else.
