Law-GAT 2026 — the Law Graduate Assessment Test

What Law-GAT is, who can sit it, how to register, what the syllabus covers, how to use past papers, and where to check your result. A complete guide for Pakistani law graduates.

Law-GAT is the assessment test that Pakistani law graduates must pass before enrolling as an advocate. It is conducted by HEC with the Pakistan Bar Council and is normally held twice a year.

Higher Education Commission (HEC) with the Pakistan Bar Council

Multiple-choice questions across core legal subjects

Who is eligible to sit the Law-GAT?

Candidates need a recognised LLB or equivalent degree, identity documents and the prescribed fee. Final-year students can often apply, but confirm the current notification.

  • A recognised LLB, or an equivalent degree accepted by the Pakistan Bar Council
  • CNIC and degree or transcript details
  • Registration fee for that cycle, paid through the route stated in the notification
  • Foreign degree holders must first complete HEC equivalence
Do this first: read the current notification on HEC's official website. Do not trust dates forwarded on WhatsApp or published on blogs, including this page.

What does the syllabus cover?

The test centres on core subjects: constitutional law, criminal law and procedure, civil law, the law of evidence and family law, plus legal English.

SubjectWhat to revise
Constitutional lawFundamental rights, writ jurisdiction, key constitutional provisions
Criminal lawCore PPC offences (qatl, hurt, property) and CrPC procedure
Civil lawCPC (plaint, jurisdiction, appeals), Contract Act, Specific Relief
Law of evidenceQanun-e-Shahadat 1984: relevancy, burden of proof, documents
Family lawMFLO 1961, dissolution of marriage, custody, maintenance
Legal EnglishComprehension, legal terminology, drafting basics

What is a realistic 60-day preparation plan?

Spend the first ten days on bare Acts, the next fifteen on subject-wise practice questions, then past papers under time pressure, and the final week on revision only.

  1. Days 1–10: read the bare Acts for PPC, CrPC and CPC. Turn key sections into flashcards.
  2. Days 11–25: subject-wise practice questions. Write down the reason for every wrong answer.
  3. Days 26–40: past papers under timed conditions. Keep a list of weak areas.
  4. Days 41–52: weak areas, plus evidence and family law.
  5. Days 53–60: revision and full-length mocks. Add no new material.

Where do you check the result and answer key?

Only on the official HEC result portal, using your roll number or CNIC. Result day traffic is heavy, so retry on the official portal rather than clicking on forwarded links.

Warning: any "leaked result" link shared before the official announcement is usually phishing. Log in only through the official portal.

What happens after you pass?

You apply to your provincial bar council for enrollment, which includes a prescribed pupillage in a senior advocate's chamber.

In Sindh, the application goes to the Sindh Bar Council and includes a six-month pupillage, documents and the current fees. The full sequence is in our bar council enrollment guide.

Frequently asked questions

How many times a year is the Law-GAT held?

Usually twice a year, but HEC confirms the schedule for each cycle. Always check the official notification.

Can I become an advocate without the Law-GAT?

The prescribed test must be passed before enrollment as an advocate. Confirm the current requirement with your provincial bar council.

What if I fail?

You can normally attempt the next cycle. Use the gap to work in a chamber: drafting practice helps both the retest and the pupillage that follows.

When is the result announced?

The result timeline is set by HEC in each notification and published on its official portal.
