Cause List Tracking for Pakistani Lawyers

How Pakistani lawyers track cause lists and court dates: where cause lists are published, how to read them, a simple case-register system, and a daily routine that prevents missed hearings.

A cause list is the court's daily schedule of cases. Track it by checking the official court source for your court every evening, matching your case register against it, and confirming the result of each hearing the same day. Never depend on a client or a colleague to tell you a date.

Where are cause lists published in Pakistan?

Official court websites publish daily and weekly cause lists; district courts rely heavily on notice boards and the bar association's list, and court staff can confirm a fixed date.

  • Sindh High Court — daily cause lists are published on the court's official website, normally in the evening for the next working day.
  • District and City Courts — printed lists on the notice board, plus the list circulated at the bar room. Confirm with the reader or the court office.
  • Supreme Court — cause lists are published on the Supreme Court website for each bench.
  • Special courts and tribunals — check with the office directly; publication online is not guaranteed.
Verification rule: treat any date received by WhatsApp as unconfirmed until you have seen it on the official list or been told by the court office. Screenshots get edited and forwarded with the wrong date.

How do you read a cause list quickly?

Find your court and bench first, then scan for the case number — not the party names, which are often spelled inconsistently.

  1. Confirm the court, bench and judge heading. The same case can appear before a different bench.
  2. Search the case number and year, then cross-check the parties.
  3. Note the serial number, the purpose (arguments, evidence, order) and the time slot if shown.
  4. Mark whether the matter is listed as fixed or in the supplementary list, which is often heard later in the day.
  5. Write the serial number on your file cover before you leave the chamber.

What should a simple case register contain?

One line per case with the court, file number, parties, next date, purpose, fee position and what happened at the last hearing.

ColumnWhy it matters
Court, bench, judgeDecides where you stand at 9 a.m.

Last order, one linePrevents repeating an argument already decided
Fee and payment statusStops you appearing on an unpaid brief
Documents dueEnsures the file is complete before the date

A spreadsheet is enough. The point is one register, updated the same day, not five notebooks and a phone gallery.

What is a workable daily routine?

Evening: check tomorrow's lists against the register. Morning: re-check, then report each hearing result before you leave court.

  • 6 p.m. — open the official list for each court where you have a matter tomorrow.
  • 6.30 p.m. — update the register, message the client the time and court, and prepare the file the night before.
  • 8.30 a.m. — re-check the supplementary list before entering the courtroom.
  • After each hearing — write the one-line order and the next date in the register, not on a loose paper.
  • Weekly — back up the register and review matters with no date for more than 30 days.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if a lawyer misses a hearing?

The court may pass an ex parte order, dismiss the matter for non-prosecution, issue warrants, or impose costs. Recovery usually needs an application for restoration or recall, which costs time and money.

Can a client check the cause list themselves?

They can read the public list, but the responsibility for tracking stays with the advocate. Set the expectation at the first meeting: dates are confirmed by the chamber, not by rumour.

Is there one app for all Pakistani cause lists?

No single official app covers every court. Practitioners use the individual court websites plus their own register. Any third-party app should be treated as a convenience, never as the source of truth.
