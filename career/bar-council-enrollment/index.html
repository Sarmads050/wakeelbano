Bar Council Enrollment — the Full Route to Sanad

How advocate enrollment works in Pakistan: eligibility under the 1973 Act, documents, the six-month pupillage, fees, the enrollment committee and licence verification.

Enrollment as an advocate requires a recognised LLB, the prescribed test, a six-month pupillage in a senior advocate's chamber, the documents and fees, and clearance by the provincial bar council's enrollment committee.

Who is eligible for enrollment?

A Pakistani or AJK citizen, at least 21 years old, holding a recognised LLB or Bar-at-Law, who has passed the prescribed test and meets the character requirements.

  • Citizenship of Pakistan or Azad Jammu and Kashmir
  • Minimum age of 21 years
  • LLB or Bar-at-Law recognised by the Pakistan Bar Council
  • Prescribed test passed, under Pakistan Bar Council rules
  • No disqualification under sections 26 and 27 of the 1973 Act

Holders of foreign degrees must first complete HEC and Pakistan Bar Council equivalence.

What is the step-by-step process?

Pass the Law-GAT, complete six months of pupillage, prepare the document set, apply to the provincial bar council with the fees, appear before the enrollment committee, then collect the certificate and identity card.

  1. Law-GAT — pass the test (see the Law-GAT guide).
  2. Pupillage — six prescribed months in a senior's chamber.
  3. Documents — LLB degree and transcripts, CNIC, photographs, character certificate, test result and the prescribed forms.
  4. Application — file with the Sindh Bar Council in Sindh, with the enrollment fee, benevolent fund and other charges set by its rules.
  5. Enrollment committee — review and interview.
  6. Certificate and identity card — issued on approval.
Figures change: fee amounts, challan routes and checklists are revised from time to time. Confirm the current requirements at the bar council office or website, not on a blog.

Why is pupillage the hardest step?

Because there is no placement system. Pupillage is mandatory, but finding a chamber still depends almost entirely on personal contacts.

This is the gap WakeelBano exists to close. Practical ways to make pupillage count:

  • Take a one-page CV to a chamber visit: what you want to learn, how much time you can give, which area interests you
  • Agree the terms at the start: hours, type of work, any stipend, and what you will be taught
  • Write one page of notes every day — six months later that record is worth more than the degree
  • Browse verified chambers in Chamber Match on WakeelBano

How do you verify an advocate's licence?

Search the provincial bar council roll by name or registration number. In Sindh, the Sindh Bar Council publishes an advocate verification portal showing district, licence type and status.

Clients and employers should always verify against the roll rather than a screenshot. It is the simplest defence against imposters, an issue the Sindh Bar Council has publicly flagged more than once.

What should you do in the first 90 days after enrollment?

Commit to one court and a few case types, start a practice record, build a clean public profile, and tell twenty people in person what you do.

  • Fix your court, area and case types — spreading thin does not generate income
  • Record every appearance and file, because the High Court licence will need that record
  • Set up a LinkedIn profile, WhatsApp Business and a verified listing
  • Tell your network directly: most first briefs come from referrals, not advertising

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to become an advocate in Pakistan?

A five-year LLB, the Law-GAT, six prescribed months of pupillage and the enrollment process: roughly six years in total, depending on individual timing.

Is the pupillage paid?

Not necessarily. Some chambers pay a stipend and many do not, which is why agreeing the terms in writing at the start is important.

How much are enrollment fees?

The enrollment fee, benevolent fund and other charges are set by bar council rules and are revised over time. Ask the Sindh Bar Council for the current challan and breakdown.

Can I practise in another province after enrolling in Sindh?

Practice rights follow your enrollment and licence type. Confirm the position with the bar council concerned before taking instructions outside your province.
