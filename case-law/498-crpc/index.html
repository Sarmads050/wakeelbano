498 CrPC — Pre-Arrest Bail

How pre-arrest bail works in Pakistan: the exceptional nature of the relief, the grounds, the documents, what to file before the High Court, and the mistakes that get it refused.

Pre-arrest bail under section 498 CrPC is an exceptional remedy. The applicant must show that the case is mala fide, that he has been implicated to harass him, or that there are exceptional circumstances — and courts treat it as a last resort, not as a routine step after an FIR.

Key facts

Statute
Criminal Procedure Code 1898, section 498
Nature
Exceptional and discretionary — not a matter of right
Grounds
Mala fide, ulterior motive, no reasonable likelihood of arrest, or exceptional circumstances
Forum
Sessions court or High Court, depending on the case and stage
Key documents
FIR, applicant's account, and material showing the motive
Risk
An application that fails can smooth the way for arrest

When does pre-arrest bail actually succeed?

When the applicant shows the case itself is an abuse of process, or that the facts do not fit the offence alleged at all.

  • Mala fide — the FIR follows a property dispute, a family dispute or a refusal to pay, and the criminal case is pressure.
  • No connection to the offence — the applicant is named but the material shows he was elsewhere or had no role.
  • Delay and conduct — the applicant appears before the court voluntarily and has not absconded.
  • Exceptional circumstances — age, health, or a purely civil dispute dressed as a crime.
Honest advice to the client: pre-arrest bail is not a routine step. If the facts genuinely attract the offence, an unsuccessful application weakens the later regular bail application.

What should the file contain?

The FIR, the applicant's own account, the documents behind the dispute, and anything that shows when and why the complaint appeared.

  1. Certified copy of the FIR, with the date and the police station.
  2. The applicant's account, in an affidavit, dated and specific.
  3. The underlying documents — the sale agreement, the cheque, the family dispute, the prior civil suit.
  4. Evidence of the motive — messages, prior complaints, the timeline of the dispute.
  5. Any order already passed in related proceedings.
  6. A short chronology, because these applications are decided on the story the documents tell.

What are the procedural steps?

File before the appropriate court, serve notice, argue interim protection, and expect conditions on any relief granted.

  • Forum — sessions court or High Court, depending on the offence and where the case is pending.
  • Interim protection — ask for protection until the next date, or the purpose of the application is defeated.
  • Conditions — courts usually impose conditions such as joining the investigation and not leaving the jurisdiction.
  • Duration — relief is normally time-bound, and the applicant must then seek regular bail.
  • Compliance — a breach of any condition is itself a reason to cancel the relief.

Frequently asked questions

Is pre-arrest bail a right?

No. It is an exceptional discretionary relief, granted where the process of law is being misused. Regular bail after arrest is a different application with different tests.

Can it be filed directly in the High Court?

It can, but practice varies with the case and the stage. Follow the route used in your own court and check the current rules before filing.

What happens if the application is dismissed?

The applicant loses the protection of the court and the arrest may follow. That is why the documents — not the drafting — decide whether to file at all.
