489-F PPC — Cheque Dishonour

Section 489-F PPC explained: the offence of dishonestly issuing a cheque, the elements to prove, complaint preparation, common defences and how to research the case law.

Section 489-F of the Pakistan Penal Code deals with dishonestly issuing a cheque that is returned unpaid, where the person knew the funds were insufficient. Punishment includes imprisonment and a fine.

Key facts

Statute
Pakistan Penal Code 1860, section 489-F
Core element
Dishonest intention when the cheque was issued
Key documents
Original cheque, bank return memo, proof of the debt, notice
Searched as
“489-F case law”, “cheque bounce case Pakistan”

What must be proved in a 489-F case?

That the cheque was issued, that it related to a debt or obligation, that it was dishonestly issued, that it was returned unpaid, and that a demand was made.

ElementEvidence usually relied on
IssuanceThe original cheque, signature and bank record
Debt or obligationAgreement, invoice, receipt, message trail
Dishonest intentionKnowledge of insufficient funds, account history, conduct
DishonourThe bank's return memo, with the reason stated
DemandThe notice and proof that it reached the other side

What is the pre-complaint checklist?

Secure the original cheque, obtain the return memo, assemble proof of the underlying debt, send a demand notice and confirm the limitation period and the correct forum.

  1. The original cheque, plus copies
  2. The bank return memo — the stated reason matters enormously
  3. Proof of the debt: agreement, invoices, ledgers, messages
  4. A legal notice with proof of delivery
  5. The limitation period, checked against the section and current case law
  6. The competent court, determined by territorial jurisdiction

What defences are commonly raised?

That the cheque was given as security rather than for a debt, that no debt is proved, that the signature is not the client's, or that the dispute is essentially civil.

  • The cheque was a security cheque, not payment for a debt
  • The underlying debt is not proved
  • The signature is disputed
  • The element of dishonest intention is missing
  • The demand procedure was not followed
  • A civil dispute has been given a criminal colour

Research key words: "489-F PPC", "security cheque", "dishonest intention", "quantum of sentence", "compounding".

Should the client pursue criminal, civil, or both?

It depends what the client wants: punishment or recovery. Many matters end in a recorded settlement, and the client should decide the objective before filing.

Criminal and civil remedies run on different tracks with different outcomes and timelines. Explain both clearly, including the cost of each, before the client commits.

Frequently asked questions

What is the punishment under 489-F PPC?

The section provides imprisonment and a fine. Read the bare Act for the exact wording and check current authority, since sentencing depends on the facts.

What is a security cheque?

A cheque handed over as a guarantee rather than to discharge a debt. That distinction affects whether dishonest intention can be established, so research the case law carefully.

Can a cheque case be settled?

Yes, frequently. Any settlement should be recorded in writing and given effect through the court's process.
