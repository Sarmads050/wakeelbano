Child Custody in Pakistan

How custody works in Pakistan: hizanat, the welfare-of-the-child principle, ages and guardianship, visitation rights, and the evidence a custody case actually turns on.

In Pakistan the mother normally has the right of hizanat (physical custody) of young children, while the father remains the natural guardian. Every custody decision is ultimately decided on the welfare of the child, not on the parents' wishes.

Key facts

Statutes
Guardians and Wards Act 1890; Family Courts Act 1964
Mother's hizanat
Generally until a son is about 7 and a daughter reaches puberty, subject to the court's view of welfare
Guardianship
The father is normally the natural guardian for property and legal acts
Deciding test
Welfare of the minor, above the parents' rights
Visitation
The court fixes times and can enforce them
Evidence
School records, living arrangements, income, conduct, medical and support history

What does the welfare principle mean in practice?

The court asks who can provide stability, schooling, care and a safe home — and it can depart from the usual age rules if the child's welfare requires it.

  • Continuity of school and neighbourhood.
  • Who has actually been caring for the child day to day.
  • Housing, income and the ability to supervise.
  • Any risk of harm, neglect or exposure to conflict.
  • The child's own wishes, given weight according to age and understanding.
Common mistake: treating custody as a prize to be won. Courts respond to a plan for the child's routine, not to allegations against the other parent.

Can the mother lose custody?

Yes, if the court finds that the child's welfare requires it — for example where she remarries a person who is a stranger to the child, or cannot provide care, though each case is decided on its own facts.

Hizanat is a right that yields to welfare. Remarriage, relocation, neglect, or a home where the child cannot be properly supervised are all factors a court will examine. So are the father's conduct and his capacity to care for a young child.

How is visitation handled?

The court fixes specific days and times, and can vary them; a party who obstructs visitation risks an adverse view in the custody decision.

  1. Ask for defined visitation in the decree, not a general statement of access.
  2. Set out days, times, place of pick-up and return, and overnight arrangements.
  3. Keep a written log of every visit and every missed visit.
  4. If an order is breached, apply to the court promptly with the log.

Frequently asked questions

At what age does a boy go to the father?

The usual guidance is around seven years for a son, with the court free to decide otherwise on welfare. Always argue welfare, not just the age rule.

Can a custody order be changed later?

Yes. A change in circumstances — schooling, remarriage, relocation, the child's own wishes — can justify a fresh application.

Does custody decide guardianship too?

No. Physical custody (hizanat) and legal guardianship are separate questions, and the father usually remains the natural guardian.
