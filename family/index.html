Family Law in Pakistan

Plain-language guides to Pakistani family law: khula and divorce, maintenance, child custody, dower and the Family Courts procedure. Written for lawyers and for the client sitting in front of them.

Family cases in Pakistan run under the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961, the Family Courts Act 1964 and the Guardians and Wards Act 1890. Almost every family suit begins with a family court and a mandatory pre-trial reconciliation.

Which court hears a family case?

The family court established under the Family Courts Act 1964, sitting in the district where the parties reside or the cause of action arises.

Family courts deal with dissolution of marriage, dower, maintenance, restitution of conjugal rights, custody, guardianship, and the return of bridal gifts. A family suit is filed on a prescribed form, not an ordinary plaint, and the court must attempt reconciliation first.

Practical point: a family suit that is filed as an ordinary civil suit wastes months before it is returned at the scrutiny stage. Check the form and the court fee before you file.

How long does a family case take in Karachi?

A contested khula or maintenance matter often runs from one to three years through the family court and the appeal, while an uncontested one can close in a few months.

Duration depends on service of notices, reconciliation attempts, evidence and adjournments. The single biggest factor you control is completeness of the file: correct addresses, CNIC copies, nikah nama, and a clear list of documents.

Frequently asked questions

Can a woman file for khula without the husband's consent?

Yes. Khula is a dissolution sought by the wife; the court decides it on the wife's statement, and the husband's consent is not a legal condition, though the court considers the return of dower. Confirm the current practice in your court before advising a client.

Are family cases heard in camera?

Family courts generally sit in camera and publication of proceedings is restricted. Follow the court's direction in your own matter.
