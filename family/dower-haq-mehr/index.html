Haq Mehr (Dower) in Pakistan

Dower in Pakistani law: prompt and deferred mehr, how it is recorded in the nikah nama, how it is recovered, and how it interacts with khula and the wife's other claims.

Dower (haq mehr) is a mandatory payment from the husband to the wife under the nikah, either prompt (mu'ajjal) or deferred (mu'ajjal). It is the wife's own property, can be recovered through the family court, and is not cancelled automatically by a khula decree.

Key facts

Nature
A legal obligation, not a gift or a formality
Two kinds
Prompt mehr, payable on demand; deferred mehr, payable on death or dissolution
Where recorded
The nikah nama, in figures and words
Recovery forum
Family court, usually with the other matrimonial claims
Effect of khula
The court considers whether mehr should be returned; it is not automatically forfeited
Interaction
Independent of maintenance, which is a separate right

Prompt or deferred — why the distinction matters

Prompt mehr can be demanded immediately; deferred mehr becomes payable on the dissolution of the marriage or the husband's death.

Prompt (mu'ajjal)Deferred (mu'ajjal)
When payableOn demand, after the nikahOn dissolution or death
Common practicePart of the mehr paid at marriageThe larger part, recorded in the nikah nama
Risk to the wifeMay be waived if not claimed earlyRecoverable from the estate
Drafting point: if the nikah nama records a figure in words and a different figure in digits, expect a dispute. Check both before you file.

How is mehr recovered?

By a suit in the family court, usually pleaded together with maintenance and the dissolution, and proved by the nikah nama and any receipts.

  1. Read the nikah nama carefully: amount, kind (prompt or deferred), and any conditions.
  2. Plead the mehr claim in the same suit as dissolution or maintenance.
  3. Attach the nikah nama and any evidence of part payment.
  4. Ask for a clear decree amount, not a formula.
  5. Plan enforcement at the time of filing, not after the decree.

What happens to mehr in a khula?

The court considers whether the wife should return the mehr as a condition of the dissolution; the classic position is that khula involves a return, but the court has discretion on the facts.

Do not tell a client that mehr is automatically lost, and do not tell her it is automatically kept. Plead the financial position, the length of the marriage and the reason for the breakdown, and let the court decide the terms. Record the outcome in the decree in figures.

Frequently asked questions

Can mehr be waived?

A wife may give up her mehr, but any waiver should be in writing and understood. Courts look closely at waivers signed under pressure.

Is mehr payable if the marriage was never consummated?

The position depends on the facts and the kind of mehr. Plead the specific facts; do not generalise.

Does the widow inherit mehr from the estate?

Deferred mehr is a debt on the estate and is dealt with alongside the inheritance claim. Handle both together.
