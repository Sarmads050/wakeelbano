Free Legal Research Resources in Pakistan

Where Pakistani lawyers can research for free: official court websites, bar council registers, bare statutes, government notifications, and how to verify a citation before you rely on it.

A Pakistani lawyer can research for free using the Supreme Court and High Court websites for judgments and cause lists, the provincial bar council registers to verify an advocate, the bare statutes for the text of the law, and government websites for notifications, rules and fees. Always open the report or the statute itself before you cite it.

Where can you read judgments for free?

Start with the court websites for recent reported judgments; use the printed reports in a chamber or bar library for older authority, because a citation must match the report.

SourceWhat it is best for
Supreme Court of Pakistan — supremecourt.gov.pkJudgments and cause lists of the Supreme Court
Sindh High Court — sindhhighcourt.gov.pkJudgments, cause lists and district judiciary information
Printed reports — PLD, SCMR, CLD, MLDThe version a court will actually check. A web copy is not a substitute.
Chamber and bar association librariesOlder volumes, and the only reliable place to complete a citation
Practice rule: if you have only seen a judgment quoted inside another judgment or a blog, you have not read it. Trace it to the report, note the page, then use it.

How do you verify that someone is really an advocate?

Through the provincial bar council register or verification portal, using the enrolment number and name.

  • Sindh Bar Council — verification portal at advocates.sindhbarcouncil.org for advocates enrolled in Sindh.
  • Pakistan Bar Council — for rules, enrolment framework and council notices.
  • Other provinces — Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan and Islamabad bar councils each maintain their own register.
  • What to record — roll number, date of enrolment, and the bar council that issued it. A name alone proves nothing.

Where do you find the text of a statute?

Bare Acts for the code itself, and the government website for the amendments and notifications that change it.

  • Bare Acts — PPC, CrPC, CPC, Qanun-e-Shahadat 1984, Contract Act, Specific Relief Act, Limitation Act, Family Courts Act, MFLO 1961.
  • Federal law — the National Assembly and the Ministry of Law publish Acts and ordinances.
  • Provincial law — the Sindh government website publishes Sindh Acts and labour notifications, including minimum wage.
  • Rules and schedules — often issued separately from the Act, and often what a court applies in practice.
Warning: a bare Act printed five years ago may not contain the amendment that decides your case. Check the latest notification before you draft.

Where are cause lists and court dates published?

On the court's own website for the High Court and Supreme Court, and on the notice board and bar room list for district courts.

  • Supreme Court — cause lists published per bench on the court website.
  • Sindh High Court — daily lists, normally published the evening before.
  • District and City Courts — printed lists on the notice board, confirmed with the court office.
  • Rule — treat any date received on WhatsApp as unconfirmed until it appears on the official list.

What is the four-step method we recommend?

Chronology, provision, authority, verification — in that order, every time.

  1. Chronology — every date, document and payment on one page before you read any law.
  2. Provision — the exact section that governs the point, read in the current amended form.
  3. Authority — judgments on the same fact pattern, not the same section number.
  4. Verification — open each authority in the report, note the page, and check it has not been overruled.

Frequently asked questions

Is all of this really free?

Yes. Court websites, bar council registers and government notifications are public. The printed reports are not free, which is one reason chamber and bar libraries still matter.

Can I rely on a judgment I found on a blog?

No. Blogs, including this one, are for orientation. Trace every authority to the report before you cite it in court.

How do I check whether a judgment has been overruled?

Read the later judgments that cite it, and check the current edition of the report. A case that has been overruled is worse than no citation at all.

Do you offer a case law search engine?

No. Several Pakistani portals already do that well. We explain how to use the official sources and how to verify what you find, and we publish focused guides on the sections lawyers search most.
