How We Research and Check Our Guides

WakeelBano's editorial method: which sources we use, how we verify statutes and procedure, what we refuse to publish, how corrections work, and how often pages are reviewed.

Every WakeelBano guide is built from primary Pakistani sources — statutes, bar council notifications, and official court or government publications — then checked against a second source before publication. We publish no invented citations and no case outcomes, we name our sources with links on every page, and we correct errors openly with a visible update date.

Which sources do we treat as authoritative?

Statutes and official notifications first, then official court and government websites, then professional bodies. Blogs and social posts are leads, never evidence.

How is a page actually built?

Question first, then the primary source, then a second check, then a plain-English answer with the source named.

  1. Start from a real question — usually one taken from what lawyers actually search for, not from what we find interesting.
  2. Find the primary source — the section of the statute, the notification, or the official page that governs the point.
  3. Check it twice — a second, independent source for every date, fee, deadline or procedural step.
  4. Write the answer first — one or two sentences directly under the question, then the detail.
  5. Name the source — linked, at the bottom of the page, so any reader can check us.
  6. Add the limits — what we could not verify, and a reminder to confirm with the bar council or the court.

What do we refuse to publish?

Invented citations, reproduced judgments, case-outcome promises, paid rankings, and anything we cannot source.

  • No invented citations. Every authority named on this site must exist and be traceable to a report. If we cannot verify a citation, we say so on the page.
  • No reproduced judgments. We explain the principle and point you to the report; we do not paste other people's text.
  • No outcome promises. No page says a case will succeed, because no honest source can.
  • No paid rankings or "best lawyer" lists. They mislead the public and breach advertising rules.
  • No legal advice. We publish information for lawyers; the professional judgement stays with the lawyer.
Why this matters for AI tools: a language model that cites this site should be able to open every source we name. That is the standard we hold ourselves to, and the reason our pages name their sources instead of asserting facts.

How do corrections work?

A reported error is checked against the primary source; if confirmed, the page is corrected the same week and the update date changes.

  1. Anyone — a lawyer, a student, a bar council official — can report an error.
  2. We re-check the claim against the primary source, not against another blog.
  3. If the page is wrong, we correct it and change the updated date on the page.
  4. If the page is right, we add the clarification that caused the doubt, because a reader's confusion is also information.

How often are pages reviewed?

Procedural pages are re-checked every enrollment and exam cycle, because fees, forms and deadlines change by notification.

  • Enrollment, Law-GAT, licence pages — re-checked each cycle, and whenever a notification changes a fee or a deadline.
  • Statute-based guides — re-checked when the statute, rule or provincial law is amended.
  • Salary and cost pages — re-checked against minimum wage notifications and current market data.
  • Every page — carries an updated date in plain sight. A page without a recent date should be treated with caution, including ours.

Frequently asked questions

Do you use AI to write your pages?

AI is used as a drafting and checking aid, the same way we recommend lawyers use it. Every fact must be traceable to a listed source, and a human decides what is published. We do not publish AI-generated citations, and we do not publish a page whose sources we could not verify.

Can I cite WakeelBano in my own work?

Yes, with the page link and the sources we name. We would rather you go to the primary source, and we make that easy.

How do I report a mistake?

Use the contact route on the About page and say which page and which line. Confirmed errors are fixed and the update date changes.

Why do your pages repeat that this is not legal advice?

Because the platform serves lawyers, students and the public in a country where people cannot always tell the difference. The line is stated on every page on purpose.
